Suggested narration:

“ApronLens helps a learner follow a changing airport situation and work out what the available reports actually establish. We are showing selected excerpts from a recorded native iOS Simulator run. The exercises focus on an obstructed ground route, an arriving aircraft waiting for an occupied stand, and delayed baggage affecting a departure estimate.

The learner starts with a short briefing. During practice, they watch the aircraft, select a flight, read incoming reports and ask the assistant questions. There are three checkpoint moments in each lesson. At each checkpoint, the scene, assistant and history are hidden, and the learner answers a four-choice question. Feedback explains the answer using the reports available at that moment. The lesson ends with a debrief. The scored learner flow is separate from the guided recording used for these movement clips.

Here, TRN103 is waiting for Stand B. The important question is whether the stand is confirmed available. A plan for the occupying aircraft to move is not enough. At this answer's cutoff, the report still says the stand is occupied.

Now a later report confirms availability and releases the exercise hold. The answer changes because the evidence has changed. Selecting the flight gives the question its context, and the cutoff tells us which reports were available to answer it.

Sources opens the reports supporting that particular answer. Recent reports can include updates received afterward. That difference helps the learner check both the evidence and its timing.

The local Qwen model selects relevant report facts. A separate checker checks the claims against eligible facts, and the app displays the accepted summary. The responses in this recording came from actual requests; they were not replaced with rehearsed answers.

Next, we will look at how the moving scenario is driven and what we have evaluated.”

Driver access for Person 2 — preparation notes, not a passage to read aloud

The video shows the driver's effects: the clock, 30×/normal-speed changes, aircraft paths, incident animation and arriving reports. It does not show a separate driver console. The driver runs on the Mac, independently of the Unity viewer and the language model, and is included in the published repository.

After the repository and local model are set up, run this from the repository folder:
.venv-candidates/bin/python -m backend.training --exercise late_arrival --mode demo --seed 17

Use --mode lesson for three scored checkpoints, or --mode continuous for the hour at normal speed. Run one service at a time on port 8767. Open the configured iOS Simulator app after the model finishes loading. The model and driver stay on the Mac; the simulator is the viewer. A clone alone does not install the model, Unity or Xcode.

Access and setup: https://github.com/MayFairMI6/apron-lens/blob/codex/checkpoint1-controller-training/docs/TRAINING_DRIVER.md
Add --environment rain, --environment snow, or --environment dusk to the launch command for visual conditions. The default remains clear daytime. The driver publishes versioned time-stamped intensity/light frames; TrainingWeather.cs renders them using the shared source clock. The weather preview clips disable question/teaching pauses in a private recording harness; they add no assistant answers or scoring.

Implementation: backend/training_environment.py defines the visual timeline; backend/lessons.py creates the seeded world and reports; backend/training_session.py owns time and lesson state; backend/training.py exposes the service; ExternalTrafficDriver.cs receives it in Unity.

Recording checklist

1. Keep the embedded Person 1 audio. Record Person 2 while watching the section after the Person 2 title card, then combine the tracks.
2. Download the video before editing. Use the source-player times above. Keep the assistant answer large enough to read and leave short pauses for movement.
3. Record voice in short paragraphs while watching the selected clips. Explain what is happening, what the learner should notice, and what the evidence supports. Do not read every report aloud.
4. Use a brief labelled learner-flow capture/debrief image, then the guided excerpts. One combined video is enough. A freeze-frame for explanation is fine if its use is clear.
5. Review both sections together for repeated explanations, readable sources and accurate claims. The saved footage has genuine answers; do not replace a failed answer with scripted output.
6. Submit the final narrated edit separately from the reflection. Coordination and Meetings in the reflection remain to be completed.

Running or sharing the app

The native iOS Simulator app has been built and tested. Running it again requires both the installed app and the local scenario/model service. The repository's TEAMMATE_SETUP.md and CHECKPOINT1_CURRENT.md explain the Apple Silicon Mac, Unity, Xcode and model setup. Both people can prepare narration from the recording without installing that environment.

ApronLens is positioned as portable scenario practice and review for learners, not an operational control workstation. The phone suits a focused flight/report/checkpoint task; a tablet or larger display is the recommended format for a broader traffic overview. This is a design recommendation, not a completed controller-usability finding. Checkpoint 3 should compare phone and larger-screen use with representative learners. ENAC’s ELSA is a relevant computer/tablet training precedent: https://www.enac.fr/en/elsa-training-differently-training-anywhere

No TestFlight build has been published. The current service listens only on the original Mac's loopback address. A phone-distributable build would need device signing and an accessible authenticated service, followed by App Store Connect/TestFlight setup. For this video, the shared recording is the immediately usable route. Simulator verification does not establish the assignment's physical-smartphone requirement.
