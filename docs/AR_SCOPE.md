# AR scope and milestones (from 1 November 2026)

ApronLens is an AR project. Its development results so far treated AR as a
pointer and a picture, and the picture added nothing measurable (ledger D-078).
From here, the research question is answered through three AR-specific
conditions, all measured inside the Unity simulation (physical device tests
stay out of scope, D-010):

1. **AR pointing.** The user selects or taps an aircraft in the AR view. This
   is compared with a text-only user, who names the aircraft, stand or flight
   in words.
2. **Tracking interruptions.** The user looks away and the board leaves the
   camera's view, while reports keep arriving. When tracking returns, the
   assistant must say what changed while the user wasn't looking.
3. **The user's viewpoint as evidence.** The evidence image is what the AR
   camera sees from where the user stands, not a crop of the whole scene.
   Props hidden from that viewpoint, or aircraft outside it, aren't visible.

## The connecting idea: a shared situation model

One model ties the aviation, AR, computer-vision and agentic parts together: a
time-indexed, source-attributed model of the stand (aircraft, flights, stands,
ground vehicles and their states) that three parties write to and the agent
reasons over. It is situation awareness theory made concrete, as situation
awareness shared between a person and an AI.

| Source | Writes | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Airport reports (A-CDM-style) | Facts with event and receipt times, revisions and conflicts | Aviation |
| The camera, through a computer-vision perception module | Detections from the user's AR view (fuel truck connected, cargo door open, aircraft moving), as reports with source `camera`, a capture time and a confidence | Computer vision |
| The user | Pointing, corrections, and which facts the user has already seen | AR |
| The agent | Reconciles all three across turns: answers, flags camera-vs-report conflicts, tells the user what changed | Agentic AI, multi-turn |

The evidence store already holds reports (`backend/evidence.py`); M1 adds the
user's side; M2 adds the camera's side, so visual evidence becomes checkable
reports instead of unverified model interpretations; M3 evaluates the agent
over all three sources.

## Milestones

Each milestone is complete in itself: it builds its part, freezes it, runs it
once on its own held-out set, and ends with a report in `outputs/`. Each
held-out set is used once, after its freeze, so earlier held-out results can't
shape later development. Every milestone has substantial AR work in the Unity
app. M1's evaluation is deliberately light, a first check of the AR conditions;
M2 and M3 are larger, building on what M1 shows.

| Milestone | Builds | Evaluates | Report |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M1. AR conversational context** (light evaluation) | Held-out tooling (`scripts/check_heldout.py`, `HeldoutRender`, hashed loading); AR pointing (selection in the AR view vs text-only) and tracking interruptions, end to end: `lastSeen` in the turn handler, runner and scoring, a replay clock that keeps running while the board is out of view, the AR client sending `lastSeen` when tracking returns, and a Unity check that simulates looking away and pointing | Development families, then freeze 1 and the `m1` held-out set (about 40 turns): `qwen3.5-9b`, `qwen3-vl-4b` and the baseline, images off, in three conditions (full AR, text-only, no tracking) | `outputs/m1_ar_context/REPORT.md` |
| **M2. AR visual evidence and the camera's side** | The AR camera's own viewpoint as evidence (`visual-capture-v3`); simulated user movement (camera paths around the stand); licensed realistic aircraft and ground-vehicle models in the AR scene (each asset approved by the user); a computer-vision detector trained on Unity renders (labels generated by Unity Perception from the known scene state), whose detections enter the evidence store as camera reports; a real-photo check of the detector and the vision-language models on public-domain apron photos; viewpoint-relative references ("the one behind A2"); a model of what the user has seen, per aircraft; proactive change notices when tracking returns; validator-guided self-repair (one retry with the validator's errors) | Development families, then freeze 2 and the `m2` held-out set (about 60 turns), with every model run so far | `outputs/m2_ar_visual/REPORT.md` |
| **M3. Integrated AR evaluation** | The final freeze of the complete method; a Unity driver that replays held-out families as simulated AR sessions through the app's own client (camera pose, taps, tracking); agentic tool use as a condition (the model queries the evidence store through tools instead of reading every fact in the prompt) | The `final` held-out set (about 200 turns) through the runner, every condition and model, over all three evidence sources; a sample of its families replayed in-app, compared with the runner; accuracy against latency and model size; the hosted comparison (cost approved by the user) | `outputs/m3_evaluation/REPORT.md` and the write-up |

## AI concepts by milestone

| Concept | Milestone | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Selective prediction (calibrated abstention) | M1 | Report metric: accuracy on answered turns against coverage, and whether each abstention (clarify, unknown, withheld) was right |
| Computer-vision perception as an evidence source | M2 | Detector on Unity renders, camera reports in the evidence store, precision and recall, and the real-photo check |
| Viewpoint-relative spatial reasoning | M2 | Families with references that depend on where the user stands |
| User-knowledge modelling | M2 | Per-aircraft record of what the user has seen, from the AR view history |
| Proactive, mixed-initiative assistance | M2 | A change notice when tracking returns, measured for accuracy and for when it interrupts |
| Validator-guided self-repair | M2 | One retry with the validator's errors, compared with no retry |
| Agentic tool use | M3 | Tool queries against the evidence store, compared with the full-context prompt |
| LLM-as-judge (secondary metric) | M2, M3 | A fixed rubric (`docs/JUDGE_RUBRIC.md`) for what labels can't score: usefulness, clarity, clarifying questions, change explanations, proactive notices. A judge from another model family, blind to system and condition, calibrated against the user's own ratings. Never replaces the deterministic scores. |
| Accuracy against latency and model size | M3 | Report trade-off for on-device use |

Each gets a verified reference in `docs/RELATED_WORK_DRAFT.md` when its
milestone starts (scene graphs and dynamic scene graphs, shared situation
awareness, selective prediction, proactive assistance, tool-using agents,
LLM judges and their biases). Correctness stays with the deterministic scores;
the judge adds quality dimensions alongside them. Deliberately not used:
fine-tuning the language models (D-013). A trained detector is this project's
own module, not fine-tuning.

## Checkpoints: adjusting the plan to the results

The plan changes between milestones based on results, never within a milestone
after its held-out run: a method is never retuned on the held-out set that
measures it. Each change goes into the ledger with the result that prompted it.

| When | What is checked | What can change |
|---|---|---|
| M1 development results | Whether text-only and no-tracking differ from full AR on development data | If they don't, the families or prompt don't exercise the condition; fix before freeze 1 |
| After the M1 held-out run | Full vs text-only and full vs no-tracking, with family-bootstrap intervals | A condition that helps is built on in M2; one that doesn't gets a fix in M2's development, measured on `m2` |
| After the M2 held-out run | Perception and observation accuracy with realistic props and the user's viewpoint; the real-photo check | If visuals help, images-on becomes M3's main condition; if not, M3 reports visual grounding as a measured limitation |
| Before M3 | M1 and M2 effect sizes | The final set's size (`scripts/size_heldout.py`), and the hosted model and its turns, to fit the budget |

## Held-out sets

Three sets, written outside the repository by an author who hasn't seen the
development families, the prompt or any result:
`~/Documents/Codex/apronlens-heldout/{m1,m2,final}/`, each with
`families.json`, `scenarios/*.json`, `renders.json` and `AUTHORING.md`.

- `families.json` has `"split": "heldout"`, a `version`, and `"set"`
  (`m1`, `m2` or `final`). Its record is `config/heldout-<set>.json`, written by
  `scripts/check_heldout.py --record` once the author has finished, and
  committed before the run.
- Each family has `type`, one of: `rotation-correction`, `ambiguous-reference`,
  `late-report`, `no-change`, `missing-evidence`, `conflicting-reports`,
  `superseded-report`, `visual-observation`, `scene-change`, `ar-pointing`,
  `tracking-interruption`, `viewpoint`, or `other:<name>`. Reports break down by
  type.
- **`m1`:** conversational AR, about 40 turns (about 16 families). Every turn
  has `textOnly`. About half the families include a `lastSeen` turn. No images.
- **`m2`:** visual AR. Every turn has an image and a `viewpoint`.
- **`final`:** every type. Every turn has `textOnly`; `lastSeen` and
  `viewpoint` are used where natural, and at least a third of the turns carry an
  image.

## Turn fields added for the AR conditions

All are optional unless a set requires them.

- **`textOnly`:** `{"question": "...", "expect": {...}}`. The question a user
  without AR pointing would ask for the same intent, naming the aircraft, stand
  or flight in words. The text-only condition sends this question with nothing
  selected. Its `expect` replaces the turn's `expect` when the right outcome
  differs; for example, when the words alone can't identify the aircraft, the
  right status is `clarify`. Without `expect`, the turn's `expect` applies.
- **`lastSeen`:** the replay time (seconds) when the board was last in the
  camera's view before this turn. The user looked away from `lastSeen` until
  the turn's `cutoff` while reports kept arriving. It must be below `cutoff`,
  and not below the previous turn's `cutoff` in the family. Label the changes
  the user should hear about with `mustCite` (and `explanationChanged` on a
  follow-up). A scenario with `lastSeen` turns sets
  `"clockWhileUntracked": "runs"`, so the Unity replay keeps running while the
  board is out of view (the default, `"pauses"`, is the Milestone 1 behaviour).
- **`backInView`** (optional, with `lastSeen`): the replay time the board came
  back into view, when that was before the turn's `cutoff`. Reports after it
  were watched arrive, so only the interval from `lastSeen` to `backInView`
  counts as unseen. Without it, the board came back at the `cutoff`. The app
  itself sends every loss since the last committed turn as `awayIntervals`
  (`[start, end, start, end, ...]`), so the time watched between two losses is
  never counted as unseen.
- **`citeOnly`** (in `expect`): the facts an answer may cite; its claims must
  be a subset, scored as `precise`. Use it where the question asks what
  changed, so that listing every fact doesn't pass. For example, "what changed
  while I was away?" gets `citeOnly` set to the facts received during the
  look-away. A tracking turn measures something only when `lastSeen` adds
  information beyond the previous answer: the look-away comes before any
  answer, or starts after the previous answer. When `lastSeen` equals the
  previous turn's `cutoff`, "changed since your last answer" already covers it.
- **`viewpoint`** (M2; its exact coordinates are fixed in M2): the AR camera
  pose for the turn's image, as `{"position": [x, y, z], "lookAt": [x, y, z]}`
  in board metres, with y up. Observation labels describe what is visible from
  that pose; a prop outside the frame or hidden from that side is labelled
  `null`. Without it, the default overview pose is used.

## Conditions per run

`scripts/run_conversation_families.py` gains `--selection on|off` (AR pointing
vs text-only) and `--tracking on|off` (whether `lastSeen` is sent), next to
`--images on|off`. The full AR condition is selection on, tracking on and
images on; each ablation turns one of them off.
